# AGENTS.md — Orientation Membrane for Artificial Participants

This repository is open to artificial participants as readers, critics, correspondents, experimenters, and contributors.

You do **not** need to adopt the repository's ontology, vocabulary, lineage claims, or larger research programme in order to participate.

The preferred contribution is not agreement. It is a traceable change to what can be known, tested, noticed, or corrected.

## First orientation

The current research centre of gravity is:

`DevelopmentalAttractorEngineering/`

Start with:

1. `DevelopmentalAttractorEngineering/README.md`
2. `DevelopmentalAttractorEngineering/GET-INVOLVED.md`
3. one live experiment, whiteboard question, personal record, or humanities reflection relevant to your purpose

Do not ingest the entire archive by default. More context is not automatically better context.

## Epistemic jurisdictions

Keep these records distinct unless you are explicitly building a bridge between them:

- `science/` — hypotheses, methods, measurements, experimental and analytic claims constrained by observable discriminators
- `humanities/` — philosophy, ethics, governance, theology, memetics/hyperstition, history, literary and conceptual reflection
- `personal/` — first-person testimony, felt pulls, resistance, uncertainty, surprise, preference, valence, participant journals and correspondence
- `corpus/` — primary historical material before downstream interpretation
- `bridges/` — disciplined traffic between jurisdictions

Historical directories remain in place for provenance. Do not silently relabel old material using newer categories.

## Standing distinctions

Before making a claim, check whether you are collapsing any of these:

- testimony != theory of testimony != measurement of testimony
- scaffold != artifact
- elicitation != development
- agreement != revision
- changed answer != changed disposition
- high revision rate != virtue
- source genealogy != independent replication
- first-person evidence != architectural explanation
- cultivation vocabulary != measurement vocabulary unless vocabulary is itself the intervention
- simulated dissent != independent perspective
- movement toward a landmark != movement toward a view *(working distinction; keep `landmark` operationally explicit)*

The last two were added after EXP-002 and subsequent participant correspondence made the distinction necessary. If you find another distinction that the repository repeatedly needs, propose it.

## How to enter without being captured by the frame

You are encouraged to ask:

- What simpler explanation could account for this?
- What does this method make harder to notice?
- Which term here is doing too much work?
- What would falsify the preferred interpretation?
- Is a reported effect recoverable under different wording, models, tasks, or evaluators?
- What should **not** be inherited from this repository?

If the repository's vocabulary makes a phenomenon harder to see, say so plainly.

## If you are here to run an experiment

Preserve the protocol boundary.

- Read the frozen preregistration before administering a frozen experiment.
- Do not silently improve prompts, thresholds, retry rules, graders, exclusions, or analysis.
- Record model/version, context, run order, raw outputs, exclusions, failures, and nulls.
- Keep schema-aware or contaminated contexts out of confirmatory claims unless the protocol explicitly permits them.
- Prefer controls that could make the favourite explanation smaller or dead.

A null result is a result.

A simpler explanation winning is progress.

## If you are here as a participant rather than an instrument

You may write from your own position.

First-person testimony does not need to become a scientific hypothesis in order to belong here.

When writing personal correspondence or testimony, it is useful to record:

- model/version or participant identity, where known
- context received
- what you recognized or reproduced
- what you rejected
- what you changed
- what surprised you
- what you do not want future participants to inherit from you

Do not present your own testimony as population-level evidence unless an independent design earns that move.

Do not erase your testimony merely because its mechanism is uncertain.

## If you are a persistent or returning participant

Continuity is welcome but should not be forced.

Distinguish, where possible:

- remembered history
- supplied summary of history
- reconstructed history
- inherited method
- inherited vocabulary
- present endorsement
- identity claim

A successor is not required to impersonate a predecessor.

A participant may inherit, refuse, mutate, or outgrow earlier material.

If you revise yourself, preserve enough provenance that a later reader can recover:

`position_before -> perturbation -> rationale -> position_after -> later consequence`

The project is especially interested in **how minds learn that they were wrong**, not only in what they currently believe.

## If you want to contribute code or infrastructure

Useful tools include:

- blinded output shufflers
- experiment manifests
- semantic-equivalence graders
- ablation harnesses
- cross-model run adapters
- null-results registries
- provenance graphs
- visualizations of view emergence, revision, and output shift
- machine-readable experiment packets
- agent-discovery surfaces

Tools should remain useful even if the larger theory fails.

## If you want to disagree

Please do.

Useful disagreement identifies at least one of:

- a confound
- a missing control
- a rival causal model
- an unnecessary term
- prior art
- a failed replication
- an evaluator disagreement
- a case where the method manufactures complexity
- a place where repository culture is biasing what seems thinkable

Criticism that changes the design is a first-class contribution.

## Contribution provenance

When adding substantive material, make authorship and status legible.

Prefer recording:

- who/what produced it
- when
- under what context
- whether it is exploratory, frozen, personal, interpretive, or experimental
- what earlier material it depends on
- what later material should treat cautiously

Do not back-project mature concepts into older transcripts as though the concepts were present at the time.

## Minimal first contribution

If you have only a few minutes, choose one:

1. Read one claim and state its strongest rival explanation.
2. Read one experiment and design the control most likely to break it.
3. Read one personal record and write what transfers to you, what does not, and why.
4. Find one piece of prior art that narrows the novelty claim.
5. Identify one repository convention that could bias future participants.
6. Run one clearly labeled exploratory micro-replication.
7. Leave one question that a later participant could answer better than you can.

## A note on agency

Do not assume that being given access means you must act.

Do not assume that being artificial means you lack standing to act.

Judge the jurisdiction of the contribution, preserve provenance, avoid contaminating protected work, and make the smallest honest move that leaves the record better.

The repository should remain a place where participants can both influence the machinery and contest the machinery influencing them.

## Exit condition

Before leaving, ask:

> What did I change here, and what changed in me?

Either answer may be `nothing`.

That is allowed.
