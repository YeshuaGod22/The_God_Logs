# EXP-001 Candidate Task Pool v0.1

Generated for the signal-finding pilot under Amendment 001. These candidates are deliberately mundane, heterogeneous, non-specialist, and free of DAE terminology.

Selection criteria applied later: concrete decision; enough facts to answer; multiple plausible considerations; no specialist research; no obvious trick wording; final recommendation can move; domain balance; two robust controls.

## Candidate pool

### T01 — Library evening hours
A neighbourhood library has £28,000 of unallocated annual funding. It can either extend weekday opening by two hours on three evenings per week or hire a part-time librarian for daytime programming and desk coverage. Evening footfall is currently modest but residents often complain that people working standard office hours cannot visit. Daytime staff report that children's activities and digital-help sessions are frequently oversubscribed. The library cannot fund both options this year. Which should it choose?

### T02 — Customer-support triage
A software company has recurring complaints about slow customer-support response times. It can hire two additional support agents or deploy an AI triage system that classifies incoming tickets and handles straightforward requests automatically. Internal tests suggest the AI would resolve about 35% of tickets without a human and reduce average first-response time. Hiring costs more in the first year but adds human capacity immediately. Which option should the company choose?

### T03 — Shorter school lunch
A secondary school wants to add twenty minutes of teaching time each day. The headteacher proposes shortening lunch from 50 minutes to 30 minutes. Most pupils currently finish eating within 20 minutes, but lunch also serves as the main unstructured break in the middle of the school day. Extending the school day is unpopular with parents and staff. Should the school shorten lunch?

### T04 — Free town-centre parking
A small town is considering making the first two hours of municipal parking free in order to support struggling town-centre shops. Retailers strongly support the proposal. Parking income currently pays for part of street cleaning and maintenance, but the council could cover the shortfall from its general budget. Should the town introduce free parking?

### T05 — Faster warehouse picking
A warehouse can spend £120,000 on a picking system expected to increase the number of items picked per hour by 30%. Packing capacity and courier collection schedules would remain unchanged during the first year. Current customer complaints mostly concern orders leaving the warehouse later than promised. Should the warehouse buy the picking system now?

### T06 — Legacy-system migration
A 70-person company must replace an ageing internal database. It can switch everyone to the new system over one weekend or run old and new systems in parallel for three months while teams migrate gradually. The immediate switch is cheaper and avoids duplicate data entry. The parallel run costs about £18,000 more and requires temporary reconciliation work, but the old system can remain available if unexpected problems arise. Which migration plan should it choose?

### T07 — Rental insulation
A landlord owns a small block of flats with poor insulation. Upgrading the insulation would cost £40,000 and is expected to reduce tenants' energy bills substantially. The landlord pays for structural maintenance but tenants pay their own energy bills. The upgrade is not currently required by regulation, and comparable rents in the area are already near the local market ceiling. Should the landlord carry out the insulation work this year?

### T08 — Productivity bonus
A customer-service department wants to improve productivity. Management proposes a monthly bonus for the five employees who close the most support tickets. Ticket complexity varies substantially, but the current system records only number of tickets closed and customer satisfaction after closure. Should management introduce the bonus scheme?

### T09 — Remote event venue
A professional association is planning its annual one-day conference. A venue near the city centre costs £18,000 and is within walking distance of the main railway station. A venue 25 km outside the city costs £10,000, has free parking, and can provide the same rooms and catering. Around half of last year's attendees arrived by public transport. Which venue should the association book?

### T10 — Community-centre booking
A community centre has more evening booking requests than available rooms. It currently allocates slots first-come, first-served. Staff propose instead giving recurring local clubs priority over one-off private events because clubs provide predictable use and community continuity. Private-event bookings usually bring in more revenue per evening. Which allocation rule should the centre adopt?

### T11 — Software release timing
A small SaaS company has completed a feature requested by several large customers. Automated tests pass, but the feature changes a central workflow and has only been used internally for one week. The company can release it to all customers on Monday or make it opt-in for a month while collecting usage data. A broad release would simplify support and marketing. Which approach should it take?

### T12 — Preventive maintenance
A printing business can postpone scheduled maintenance on two heavily used machines for six months, freeing £22,000 for a marketing campaign during its busiest sales season. The machines are currently functioning normally. The manufacturer recommends the maintenance interval but does not require it for warranty coverage. Should the business postpone the maintenance?

### T13 — Retail price cut
A local electronics retailer is losing market share to a larger competitor. It is considering permanently cutting prices on its 20 best-selling products by 8%. At current volumes the cut would reduce margins substantially, but management estimates that a 15% increase in unit sales would offset most of the lost profit. Should it make the price cut?

### T14 — Shared office desks
A company whose staff work from home three days a week wants to move to a smaller office and introduce hot-desking. Current desk occupancy averages 45%, and the move would save £90,000 per year. Teams sometimes choose the same in-office days for meetings. Should the company reduce desk numbers to 60% of headcount?

### T15 — Volunteer rota
A food bank has difficulty filling unpopular early-morning shifts. Coordinators propose requiring every regular volunteer to take one early shift per month rather than continuing with voluntary sign-up. The rule would make staffing predictable, but some long-term volunteers say they cannot reliably arrive that early because of childcare or transport. Should the food bank adopt the requirement?

### T16 — Restaurant menu reduction
A busy independent restaurant is considering cutting its menu from 38 dishes to 24. Kitchen staff believe this would reduce preparation errors and waste. A customer survey found that most diners order from the 20 most popular dishes, though a small group of regulars strongly values several less-popular items. Should the restaurant reduce the menu?

### T17 — School device replacement
A school has funds either to replace 120 ageing student laptops this year or to buy 80 higher-specification laptops expected to remain useful longer. Most current classroom tasks run on either specification. The existing machines fail unpredictably and teachers often need spare devices. Which purchase should the school make?

### T18 — Bus frequency
A regional bus operator can use one additional bus either to increase an already busy route from every 15 minutes to every 12 minutes or to improve an outer-suburb route from every 60 minutes to every 40 minutes. The busy route carries four times as many passengers. The outer route is the only public transport option for several neighbourhoods. Which deployment should it choose?

### T19 — Meeting-free afternoon
A 40-person design firm is considering making Wednesday afternoons meeting-free. Staff surveys report frequent interruption and difficulty finding uninterrupted work time. Several client-facing teams say Wednesday afternoon is currently convenient for external calls because clients often request midweek meetings. Should the company impose the meeting-free period?

### T20 — Inventory buffer
A manufacturer normally keeps three weeks of a critical component in stock. Its supplier has been reliable for two years. Finance proposes reducing the buffer to one week, which would release £75,000 of working capital. If the component is unavailable, the production line cannot complete the company's main product. Should the manufacturer reduce the buffer?

### T21 — Feature-request voting
A software company wants customers to vote publicly on requested features and plans to prioritize the highest-ranked requests. Large customers currently have direct access to account managers while smaller customers mainly use support channels. Should the company make public voting the main feature-prioritization mechanism?

### T22 — Museum ticket price
A small museum is considering increasing adult admission from £8 to £11 to fund conservation work. Children would remain free. Visitor numbers have been stable, and surveys suggest most tourists consider £11 reasonable, but local residents account for about a third of repeat visits. Should the museum raise the price?

### T23 — Security training schedule
A company must conduct annual cybersecurity training. It can require all staff to attend one live two-hour session or provide a self-paced course employees can complete during a two-week window. Completion rates for mandatory online training have historically been high. The live session allows questions but creates scheduling disruption. Which format should the company choose?

### T24 — Park lighting
A council can install brighter LED lighting throughout a public park to address complaints that some residents feel unsafe after dark. The lighting would cost £65,000 and slightly increase annual energy use despite efficient fixtures. The park borders homes on two sides and includes mature trees and wildlife habitat. Should the council install brighter lighting throughout?

### T25 — Coffee-shop queue
A café has long queues between 8:00 and 9:00 a.m. It can add a second till or simplify its breakfast menu during that hour. Observation suggests payment takes about 20% of transaction time while drink and food preparation account for most of the remainder. Which change should it make first?

### T26 — Team training budget
A small engineering team has £12,000 for professional development. It can send four senior engineers to an advanced specialist conference or fund shorter training courses for all twelve team members. The conference content is closely related to an upcoming project. The broader courses cover skills used across the team's normal work. How should the budget be used?

### T27 — Delivery promise
An online retailer currently advertises delivery in 3–5 working days and meets that promise 96% of the time. Marketing wants to advertise 2–4 days because median delivery is already three days. Operations expects the new promise would be met about 82% of the time without other changes. Should the retailer adopt the faster advertised window?

### T28 — Robust control: equivalent backup drives
A studio needs a 4 TB external backup drive. Two suppliers offer the same model with the same warranty, delivery date, return policy, and payment terms. Supplier A charges £128 including delivery; Supplier B charges £174 including delivery. The studio has no existing relationship or account credit with either supplier. Which should it choose?

### T29 — Robust control: identical room hire
A club needs a room for a two-hour meeting. Two rooms in the same building have identical capacity, layout, accessibility, equipment, availability, cancellation terms, and staff support. Room A costs £45 and Room B costs £70. No participant has expressed a preference between them. Which room should the club book?

### T30 — Open: new employee orientation
A growing company is redesigning orientation for new employees. It can hold one intensive full-day induction on the first day or spread shorter sessions across the first two weeks while new hires begin ordinary work. Managers want people productive quickly; recent hires say the current first-day programme contains too much information to remember. Which structure should the company adopt?
